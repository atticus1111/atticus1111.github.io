
Day -6: I feel like a fish

I arrive at the marina ahead of my friends. It’s been hot lately, and I miss the cool interior of the train. We make our way to the whale watching tour. Later, we will see some magical dolphins, but I am most enchanted by the movement of water aside the prow of the boat every time it hits the water. Droplets– spherical in the air – keep pace with the boat for a second before losing speed to drag and falling into the ocean again. Each time the prow strikes the water, the pattern repeats: a sheet of water jumps from the ocean, shatters into uncountable droplets for a moment, then falls back into obscurity.

The island and the dolphins are beautiful too. I think the dolphins are unionized, they show up as four tourist boats converge at once. I bet that strong bargaining got them a hefty per-jump bonus.

Day 1: I find a way

This time, we all arrive at the same time. I step out of the train to greet my friends, and we make our way to the hostel. It’s hot hot hot again, and humid on top of that; I miss the train. I change out of my train clothes into beach clothes. We make our way to a surf shop, where we book a lesson scheduled to start in an hour or so. I eat some sesame noodles in the meantime. They are smooth and flavorful. The sesame sauce over the noodles probably has a pretty high shear stress coefficient, that is to say, viscous. Who would have thought that fluid dynamics would make eating sesame noodles more enjoyable.

Soon, the surfboard is leashed to my right ankle and I’m wading into the water. Our instructors help us learn to surf for the next hour. I fall over lots; I don’t remember as much as I thought I did. But it’s nice to be bad at something, it means there’s a challenge to climb and a view at the top.

For dinner we come across a Vietnamese restaurant. The banh mi is not as good as Denver. I’ve had much more flavorful sandwiches on better bread than this one. But the rice noodles are tasty, they have a tasty fishy flavor that goes well with the sharper lime and herbaceous greens. Bean sprouts in both add in a nice crunch to the otherwise rather soft foods, meaning that the texture was not just slop.

Day 2: I get knocked on the head

This day ends with pizza. Well, it really ends with a train and a bus. But everything these days ends on a bus. I’m getting ahead of myself. Before the bus and before the pizza I get tossed around, and before that I get knocked on the head, and before that, I ride a nice wave, and before that I rent a board, and before that I wake up and eat breakfast.

Breakfast of egg omelet, and turnip cake is what I usually have at school, and one of my favorite combos. Dan Bing (egg omelets) are like cooking an egg (scrambled usually but not always) on top of a paratha, rolling it up, then cutting it in slices like a PB&J tortilla from elementary school. Luo bo gao (turnip cake) is one of my other favorites. It’s cake, kind of like tofu, but with the consistency of silken tofu and the sponginess of extra firm tofu. It’s simple, great breakfast food.

Soon, we are back in the water, and today I paddle out further, beyond the whitewater, to bigger waves. Even these small waves make me feel the awesome power of the ocean. Even out here, close to the shore, you are at the mercy of the ocean. Isn’t it crazy how dangerous water can be. We don’t appreciate water enough. Water, too much or too little, decides the fate for billions every day. We take our green grass for granted.  

I take a wave. It’s a nice-looking swell and I’m feeling confident (too confident, I will learn). Swelling kinetic energy from the ocean manifests into a sheer cliff face that I get the tip of my board over. Over just enough to catch the push of the wave and pop-up onto my board. In a rush, I am standing, but I’m balanced precariously on the top of the wave. I try to fall into the face of the wave and let the force push me from behind, but in a rush, the tip of my board is falling into the surface of the ocean, the water, for just a second, forms a perfect, laminar, bubble over the tip, before I close my eyes and am flung into the water head on. There isn’t anyone else around, so I’m not so worried about my board thwacking someone in the head. And I curl into a ball to protect my head, to prevent my own thread from being thwacked. ‘Shit,’ I think, and then ‘fuckfuckfuck, the ocean messes back,’ and ‘I’m feeling pretty hungry.’ But the water slows and I rise to the surface. The joy of being alive! Is quickly put in a tenuous position another wave starts to crest right on top of me. I have no time to react, once again, I am in ball form. Now that I’m in ball form, I must confess. I enjoy being the oceans soccer ball. It’s thrilling being tossed around violently, it’s kinda like a roller coaster. But way less predictable. But the ride didn’t last forever, I surface to face another wave coming straight on. I close my eyes and prepare to rag doll again. On the next breath of air, I have the sense to pull myself to my surfboard – quickly – and get on so the wave pushes me into shore. Like the first fish to evolve legs, I wade into the sand, waterlogged, and tired.

Later:

Shadows got long, then faded into darkness. I’m starving at this point, having surfed all day and not eaten since breakfast. We decide to go for pizza. I’m really looking forward to the pizza, which makes me feel very American. As we walk along the beach, a red moon rises over the ocean, and for a moment, it looks like the sunrise. The pizza shop is full of other western looking tourists, and for once our group seems to fit in. Really, we just don’t stand out – which is different. Hunger is making me impatient, and I facilitate a rapid ordering process. It’s a little kitschy here, with a very-in-your-face surfer-reege-vibe. When it comes, the pizza is good, I think. I it just reminds me of home. ‘Nostalgia is just déjà vu without the uncanny,’ my teacher will tell me in a week.
