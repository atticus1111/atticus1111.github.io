I think busses are very romantic. When some busses idle, they make this big roaring sound. It fills every part of my body. On many idle busses, I have pressed the back of my head into the seat of the buss to feel encased in the sound. I feel the engine in my chest, and it vibrates to the tips of my fingers. Each time, I imagine sinking through the world itself and into the next realm. My body is replaced by a deep drone that goes straight to the brain. The din of the idle voice drowned out the voice of my friend. And when I replied, my voice would harmonize with the fundamental frequency of the bus. Busses hum; trains tend to scream. Some trains are particularly terrible, the L comes to mind, everything there is filled with a terrible screeching sound. Busses, for the most part have no such issue. They put me to sleep, and especially long-distance ones have a habit of putting me to sleep. When not sleeping, I look out the window and imagine that I am the main character in a movie. So maybe (in the movie in my head) I’m not looking out of a bus, but out of a submarine and at a school of fish. Watching pedestrians flash by like fish and cars like whales. Although, on a bus I’m on the inside of a fishbowl. We got fish in fishbowls all mixed up. We think we are observing the fish. On a bus (my fishbowl) you realize the opposite is true. The fish is the observer. Kinda like the difference between action and contemplation. Or immortality and eternity. SO, what I’m really trying to say is that the fish is much closer to true contemplation than we could hope to get to. Go fish huh. Fishbowls (buses that is) give us a glimpse of eternity because we they force us into inaction, we are forced to be at rest, and we are forced to be observers to the world around us. (Unless you’re on your phone or writing something as I am now). These are not typical states for us to be in. As social creatures with FOMO we are rarely in fishbowls (or buses apparently, nobody likes buses, that’s cause they aren’t fish).

I’m coming back from the theater -> bar with my friends. Before that I -> class -> poke -> national palace museum -> Ningxia. I love Ningxia, some of the best food that I have had is from there. I went back to the scallion pancake place that I went to on the first night, because the pancakes are nothing short of transcendent. I also got a couple egg yolk filled taro balls. One was savory and the other was sweet. I was expecting the savory one to be sweet, because I had had the sweet one before, and that shit is like the food of the gods. It’s kinda like a jelly filled doughnut hole, but there’s a hard crust on the outside, from being deep fried, the taro inside is fluffy and sweet, without being over the top, and the inside is molten salty custard. The first few bites are to the end of getting to the eggy center which will spill out and burn your hands if care is not taken. These are the sweet taro balls which I think about at least as much as busses, or my regrets, or my favorite movies. Wonderful shit. The savory version of the balls had a salted egg center with some fishy tasting stuff just adjacent to the center. Structurally these are same to the sweet ones, but the crust is more deeply flavored, without the same degree of sweetness, and the actual dough is not as fluffy, perhaps the gluten is more developed, than the sweet ones. Although I liked the sweet ones far more, these were still wonderful. I will have them again. Unless I die a premature death.
